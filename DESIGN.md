# Tomorro — sculptural green optimism

## Overview

A confident, tactile technology style that pairs forest-green darkness with airy white and pale botanical surfaces. Chunky condensed display lettering gives it a playful industrial force, while a clean grotesk and occasional delicate serif keep detailed content approachable. Grainy sculptural forms, luminous green accents, layered interface scenes and candid workplace photography suit collaborative business software with a substantial product story.

### Essentials

- Headlines feel like compact word sculptures: Ozik 700, uppercase, 80px/0.9 with -0.01em tracking; secondary display headings use 56px/48px rather than a conventional loose sans scale.
- The default opening is a centered promise inside a forest-green stage: 800px headline measure, 640px support measure, 192px top space, paired actions and a monochrome proof strip; a 680px grainy sculptural object breaks the right edge.
- Actions are rounded capsules, not rounded rectangles: 48px height, 28px radius and 12px 20px padding; neon green is the lead action, dark green is its inverse on pale bands.
- Color is concentrated rather than scattered: broad #122314 and #273f2b dark chapters alternate with white and #f2f5eb; #68ef3f occupies short emphasis words, buttons, dots and a few oversized proof capsules, not the entire canvas.
- Product imagery has physical staging: tilted overlapping white windows in the opening, then flat white demonstrations inside 24px translucent shells with 8px inset and 16px inner corners. A simplified real workflow with floating detail cards is the fallback when full interface media is unavailable.
- Proof becomes expressive typography: include an oversized capsule headline device using 80px condensed capitals, 104px-tall pale and neon pills, and circular portrait or speech symbols; pair it with sober 24px/32px testimonial cards.
- Tactile green atmosphere and a monumental pale closing line are signature anchors: include grain over softly shaded ring-like forms, and a #f2f5eb closing band with muted #b7bda5 display text, one neon emphasis and a dark capsule action.

## Typography

- **Aeonik** — Body, navigation, actions and sentence-case headings. Clean geometric grotesk with compact proportions and a friendly rounded rhythm. If unavailable, use **Inter**. Use 400–600; preserve the tight display tracking. Font stack: "Aeonik", "Inter", Arial, sans-serif.
- **Ozik** — Large uppercase promises and section titles. Heavy condensed display sans with softened irregular shapes and unusual compact counters. If unavailable, use **Lilita One**. Use 400 as the substitute's heavy cut; tune width to retain short dense lines. Font stack: "Ozik", "Lilita One", Arial, sans-serif.
- **Instrument Serif** — Editorial inset title and small atmospheric feature labels; italic emphasis is limited to the product-overview variant. Narrow high-contrast serif, light and literary against the blocky display face. Font stack: "Instrument Serif", Georgia, serif.

**Notes:**

- Keep display lines exceptionally tight: the 80px face has a 72px line height, while the 56px secondary heading uses 48px. Do not loosen them into standard body-sans proportions.
- Use sentence case for Aeonik headings and uppercase for Ozik; the shift separates explanation from declaration.
- The main serif is a small editorial contrast, not a general headline replacement. The 56px italic serif phrase belongs only to the compact product-overview opening.
- The shared root size follows calc(0.812695rem + 0.208117vw); rem-based descendants inherit this fluid behavior. Treat the listed recipes as the desktop target.

## Imagery and graphics

### Grainy green sculpture

**Use:** Signature atmospheric art in the opening and a major product chapter.

**Observed:** A pale olive inflated torus-like object, 680px square, is cut by the right viewport edge against deep forest. Elsewhere a smaller black ring with neon-lit inner edge floats over a grainy pale-olive-to-black field. Lighting is broad and soft, with coarse photographic noise rather than glossy chrome.

**Transfer:** Create a simple abstract ring or folded volume rather than an unrelated stock illustration. The minimum version is a shaded circular form with a deep off-center cavity and a visible fine-grain layer.

**Build:** Use an SVG torus silhouette or CSS circular mask approximately 680px across. Layer a pale olive radial fill, a black elliptical cavity displaced toward the lower left, and a soft forest shadow. Add a clipped SVG feTurbulence monochrome noise layer at low opacity over the form. For the dark ring, use black outer and inner disks with an annular neon-to-white highlight; back it with broad blurred olive fields fading to black. Match the soft glow; exact stops are not essential.

### Layered product stage

**Use:** Opening product reveal and smaller feature demonstrations.

**Observed:** White document/editor windows overlap with assistant and recommendation panels; the opening scene is tilted counterclockwise, spread across a 1024px-wide scene and faded into forest at the lower edge. Later scenes are level and simplified, on pale botanical backing, with grey text bars and a few neon highlighted fields.

**Transfer:** Show a real task from the new product, using one primary window and two floating task details. If a polished product image is unavailable, construct an honest simplified workflow with neutral text bars and colored active controls rather than a generic dashboard.

### Green grain backdrop

**Use:** Atmospheric product feature chapter and editorial inset.

**Observed:** Large hazy olive highlights curl into black recesses. Fine visible noise makes the material feel like enlarged macro photography, not a smooth digital mesh. The editorial inset isolates a close detail of the same material.

**Transfer:** Use low-frequency organic shading and a single consistent grain layer so several image sizes still feel like the same material.

**Build:** Layer two oversized blurred radial gradients using pale olive rgba(183,189,165,0.8) and forest rgba(39,63,43,0.8) over black. Position the light mass toward the upper left and a dark curved recess toward the right. Overlay SVG fractal noise with a low-opacity luminosity or soft-light blend; clip to the panel shape.

### Human work photography

**Use:** Audience tiles, customer story bands and company narratives.

**Observed:** Candid people in ordinary daylight offices, warm timber and foliage balanced by neutral clothing. Audience tiles are near-square, case-study photography fills a 792px-high band, and its lower-left text panel uses black at 16% with 24px backdrop blur. Company photography uses unadorned broad 3:2 group portraits.

**Transfer:** Choose photographs where people are engaged in work rather than posing with software. Keep natural color; reserve strong green grading for campaign media.

### Logo field

**Use:** Social proof after openings and an expansive late-page proof scene.

**Observed:** Early logos form a low horizontal strip, white on dark or pale olive on white. A later broad logo matrix fades toward white at its lower edge; a compact 480px-wide blurred statement panel sits centered over it.

**Transfer:** Use authentic partner or customer marks with unified monochrome treatment. Where marks are not available, use a typographic proof field with approved categories or verified outcomes rather than invented logos.

**Build:** Arrange marks in a loose six-column field inside 1024px. Render them in pale sage with generous row gaps. Add a white vertical gradient that remains transparent through 63% then fades to white; place a 24px-radius, 40px-padded statement panel above it with backdrop-filter blur(8px).

### Oversized trophy outline

**Only for:** recognition promotions

**Use:** A playful graphic beside formal proof.

**Observed:** A neon outline trophy is dramatically oversized and clipped on the right of the pale-green panel. Dark-green dots mark several points on the contour.

**Transfer:** Use another simple achievement symbol in the same outline-and-node language.

**Build:** Draw a single SVG trophy silhouette with rounded neon stroke about 8px wide and no fill, occupying roughly half the panel and extending beyond its right and lower boundaries. Place dark-green circular nodes about 32px across along the main curve. Keep the graphic behind the text.

### Icons

**Observed:** Small navigation chevrons are 16px filled SVGs; action icons are generally 18–24px, feature illustrations 32–40px, and proof symbols can reach 72px. Icons are white or forest green, with neon reserved for status, sparkles and key controls. Round white or forest disks frame security and category symbols.

**Transfer:** Use simple filled chevrons and play triangles for actions, restrained outlined symbols for feature concepts, and a four-point sparkle for intelligent assistance. Do not make every small icon neon.

## Section recipes

How recurring sections are built.

### First screen: Forest promise opening

**Observed:** A full-width Forest stage extends beyond the first viewport. Navigation capsule sits in the fixed 112px top zone; the centered display title begins after 192px of top space, spans an 800px measure and usually breaks over two dense lines. White capitals end with a short neon phrase. A 640px support line follows, then Primary button and a video-oriented secondary action at 16px separation. Muted trust copy and white customer logos sit below. Behind these sits the right-clipped 680px Grainy green sculpture; a tilted Layered product stage begins near the bottom edge. Within the first 900px the entire promise, support, actions, trust strip, most of the sculpture and the first sliver of product media are visible; the full interface reveal falls below.

**Transfer:** For a new product, retain the dark centered declaration, one short neon emphasis, sculptural material and an authentic task-based interface reveal. Construct the ring and simplified product windows when original media is unavailable. Feature-specific pages instead open with a pale split composition: breadcrumbs, left-aligned headline and actions, right-side workflow art. A compact overview uses product-display with one product-emphasis phrase; a company narrative opens on white with a centered display title and broad team photograph. A quote-led pricing page opens dark with a centered title and overlapping form.

**Avoid:** Do not replace this with a flat two-column SaaS hero on white, place the full interface above the headline, or shrink the art into a small illustration card.

**Observed:** Botanical band contains alternating text and product-art halves. Text uses heading at 40px, lead at 18px, a short neon-bullet list and Inverse button. Product art is about 576px by 432px, with floating white workflow details. Medium bands have about 80px vertical padding.

**Transfer:** Alternate the direction only when moving to a distinct benefit. A single testimonial inset can interrupt the list to ground the feature in experience.

### Trust and comparison

**Only for:** detailed product-overview pages

**Observed:** Small uppercase eyebrow precedes a 56px condensed heading. ## Page flow

**Storyline:** Promise → tangible product demonstration → capability chapters → human and quantified proof → practical reassurance → closing invitation and substantial footer.

### Flagship marketing landing page

1. **promise** — Centered forest opening, sculptural object, two actions and customer strip.
2. **product demonstration** — Tilted layered interface reveal followed by an atmospheric capability introduction.
3. **capability chapters** — Dark task demonstration and nested white capability panels.
4. **point of view** — Compact black editorial inset and cinematic announcement.
5. **social proof** — Audience photography, security cards, a broad customer story and expressive testimonial rail.
6. **breadth of adoption** — Expansive pale logo field with a centered statement.
7. **closing call** — Monumental pale invitation, then complete forest footer with commitment strip, four columns, badges and legal baseline.

**Transfer:** Use the flagship's material contrast and proof devices, but choose the number and order of chapters to suit the product.

### Consultative pricing or qualification page

1. **promise** — Dark centered display opening.
2. **lead qualification** — Overlapping white request form with dark title tab.
3. **value explanation** — Atmospheric capability promotion, brief commitment strip and customer logos.
4. **pricing principle** — Left explanation beside stacked pale role cards.
5. **social proof** — Expressive testimonial rail and pale-green recognition graphic.
6. **objection handling** — Large focused FAQ panel.
7. **closing call** — Monumental pale invitation followed by complete forest footer.

**Transfer:** Lead with a conversation when pricing depends on usage or role; clarify the model before adding decorative recognition.

### Focused feature landing page

2. **social proof** — Logo strip and compact editorial inset.

## Writing

Assured and optimistic, with a conversational business focus. Bold declarations are supported by concrete workflow explanations and practical reassurance rather than abstract futurism.

**Do not copy:** Do not reuse Tomorro or Oro names, logos, proprietary artwork, customer identities, quotes, awards, certifications, adoption counts, performance metrics, funding announcements or commitments. All claims, examples and proof must belong to the new product. The examples below show the pattern only; write new copy for the new product.

- **Addressing the reader:** Speak directly to the reader with you and your; use we for the team's commitments and beliefs. Name the product when explaining its concrete behavior.
- **Rhythm:** Short benefit-led headings alternate with one-sentence support lines and compact task lists. Longer explanatory paragraphs belong in FAQs and the company story; actions remain brief and literal.

| Role | Pattern | Typical length | Examples of the pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hero promise | Name the broad job and a new possibility; isolate a short enabling phrase for emphasis. | 6–12 words | “Managing Contracts in the Age of AI” |
| Capability benefit | Use a balanced contrast or a direct command describing a useful outcome. | 4–9 words | “Less paperwork, more control”<br>“Collect approvals only when necessary” |
| Supporting line | Explain what the user can do, with a clear mechanism or audience. | 15–30 words | “Define and customize your approval paths, then automate the collection of internal approvals and streamline the contractual process.” |
| Reassurance | Compress the time-to-value into a simple paired statement. | 5–9 words | “Start today, deploy next week” |
| Action | A concrete verb and clear destination or next step. | 2–5 words | “Book a demo”<br>“View open roles” |
| Editorial statement | Brief collective belief that connects technology to a human outcome. | 1–3 sentences | “They shape us and guide our decisions on a daily basis.” |

**Words and phrasing to use:** less … more …; your team; control; simple; accelerate; collaborate; from … to …; ready to use; at the right time

**Avoid:** revolutionary synergy; disrupt everything; magic; unprecedented excellence; vague superlatives without proof

**Rules:**

- Let the largest heading state an outcome; explain the mechanism below it.
- Use numbers only when a nearby caption makes their meaning clear.
- Write feature lists as observable tasks, not piles of abstract nouns.
- Keep purchase objections direct; answers should explain actual conditions and limits.
- Keep the editorial voice warmer and more collective than the product explanation.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Keep the 80px uppercase display at 72px line height, with a short neon emphasis.
- Use 800px centered title blocks and 640px opening support copy.
- Pair 48px capsule actions with a 16px gap; keep the header lead action at 56px.
- Nest 16px white demonstration panels within 24px translucent shells using 8px insets.
- Give major content bands 128px vertical padding and three-column cards 24px gutters.
- Use grainy sculptural shading at large scale, with a simple ring fallback when richer art is unavailable.
- Anchor testimonial authors below the quote, retaining 40px card padding and pale paper backing.
- Finish with a spacious forest footer including the bottom proof and legal rows.

### Don't

- Do not substitute a broad neutral grotesk for the condensed display face; dense short lines are central to the identity.
- Do not turn every heading into Ozik: explanatory capability titles use 40px sentence-case Aeonik.
- Do not spread neon across body paragraphs or large page canvases; preserve short emphasis and concentrated proof devices.
- Do not use 24px corners on every card: plain testimonials use 8px, photo captions 16px and outer shells 24px.
- Do not make translucent panels milky on a white canvas; their visible smoky material requires dark art or photography behind them.
- Do not replace the natural workplace photographs with uniformly green-filtered portraits.
- Do not use broad drop shadows for all cards; most separation comes from fill, hairlines and nested corners.
- Do not collapse the footer into a thin copyright bar; retain four link groups and a distinct lower proof area.

## Motion and responsive

### Motion

**Observed:**

- Actions commonly declare 0.2s transitions; some surfaces use 0.3s and transform 0.3s. A small set declares all 0.2s ease.
- Logo carousels declare a 300s linear infinite animation.
- The navigation changes from blending into its opening background to a visible forest capsule while remaining fixed; the late logo-field statement is sticky.

**Transfer:**

- Use the declared short transitions for capsule fill and text-color changes.
- Keep the logo rail's motion extremely slow so it remains supporting proof rather than a marquee headline.
- Preserve the fixed capsule and sticky proof statement only where they do not obscure the product explanation.

**Unknown:**

- The exact timing and easing of sculptural and on-scroll entrance animation are not established.

### Responsive

**Observed:**

- Styles declare width breakpoints at 400px, 479px, 767px, 768px, 991px, 992px, 1280px and 1440px.
- The root font-size is viewport-relative; shared rem-based proportions scale with it.

**Transfer:**

- On narrow layouts, preserve the centered promise and edge-breaking sculpture but place the product reveal below the actions.
- Stack the split feature panels and turn the nested task list into a compact vertical introduction above the demonstration.
- Let expressive headline capsules wrap into short complete phrases instead of compressing their lettering beyond recognition.
- Reduce footer columns progressively while keeping the commitment strip and legal baseline distinct.

**Unknown:**

- Mobile typography, exact column changes, navigation menu behavior and small-screen media sizing are not established. These collapse rules are design recommendations.

## How to use this guide

1. Start from the Essentials and the first-screen recipe; where the guide offers variants, use the default named for a new marketing page.
2. Plan the page before building: map your content onto the section recipes and page flow, then check the plan against the Do's and Don'ts.
3. Build with the tokens and component specs; recreate signature graphics with their build recipes instead of generic decoration, and use the named font substitutes if a font is unavailable.
4. Render the page at desktop and mobile widths, compare it with the Essentials and the Don'ts, and fix the differences before you finish.
